# BUG-020 — the loop guard's cron_next misses a one-shot wake-up in the hour clocks repeat

Found by TASK-021's round-2 full suite (2026-10-25, 00:35 UTC, the night summer time ended on this computer at 01:00 UTC; ISS-014). Reproduced by the supervisor the same night against the committed program at origin/main c025ef6.

## Symptom
tests/test_loop_guard.py::test_the_hook_input_is_read_in_its_live_forms failed at its one-shot assertion: `cron_next("%d %d %d %d *" % (minute, hour, day, month), now)` for the local time of now + 1,800 s returned None. A probe at 02:39 local summer time gave, for now + 600, 1,800, 5,400 and 9,000 s, the local times 02:49, 02:09, 03:09 and 04:09 and the results right, None, right and right: only the time in the repeated hour (02:00 to 02:59 local, the second time) was missed.

## Hypotheses
1. `cron_next` (bridge/cursor-bridge/loop-guard.py:203-215) walks a naive local datetime forward one minute at a time from `datetime.fromtimestamp(now)`; naive arithmetic moves the wall clock from 02:40 to 03:00 and on, and never shows 02:00 to 02:59 again, so a schedule in the hour standard time repeats is never matched within the eight days searched. Discriminating observation: the miss is exactly the repeated hour; a time before the fold in the first occurrence (02:49) and every time after 03:00 are found.
2. The match is made, and `t.timestamp()` of an ambiguous naive time is what fails (an exception caught somewhere, or a value outside the guard's window). Discriminating observation: a match in the first occurrence (02:49, as ambiguous a wall time as 02:09) would fail the same way. It does not: it returns the right time; and `cron_next` catches nothing, so an exception would have reached the test.

## Evidence
Hypothesis 1 holds: of four probes only the repeated-hour one failed, and the first-occurrence 02:49, ambiguous as well, was found. A second run at 01:20 UTC, inside the repeated hour itself (02:20 local standard time), showed the same cause's second face: for now + 1,800 s (02:50, the second time) `cron_next` returned 00:50 UTC, the first occurrence, an hour early (3,647 s off), because the walk matched the naive wall time and `t.timestamp()` resolves an ambiguous naive time to its first occurrence. So hypothesis 2's mechanism is real too, but only as a consequence of the walk in wall time: an instant-based walk returns the instant it visited and never converts back. The loop's own structure gives the mechanism: the starting point is the naive wall time 02:36, and every later point is that plus whole minutes in naive arithmetic, which has no fold.

## Confirmed cause
`cron_next` steps through wall-clock minutes instead of through instants. On the night clocks go back, the wall clock shows the hour 02:00 to 02:59 twice; a walk in naive wall time passes it once, at the time it starts from, and never returns to it. On the night clocks go forward the walk also visits wall times that do not exist that day (02:00 to 02:59), whose `timestamp()` Python resolves to an instant an hour off.

The repair is the walk itself: through instants, one minute apart, each read as the computer's local wall time (or the zone the caller names), so every real minute of the local clock is visited once, the repeated hour twice, the skipped hour never, and the returned time is the instant visited. Nothing is hidden by it: no retry, no widened window.

## Reproduction
tests/test_loop_guard.py::test_bug020_a_one_shot_in_the_repeated_hour_is_found: on the zone Europe/Berlin, which the caller names through the new `tz` argument, it fails today (the program takes no zone) and must pass after the fix; tests/test_loop_guard.py::test_the_hook_input_is_read_in_its_live_forms passes then at every hour of the year.
