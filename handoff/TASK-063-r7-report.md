Round 7 keeps round 6's reader and closes the holes the reviewers found in it: a find clause is checked as its own word list, cmd ends a command name where cmd ends it and refuses a word that cannot be joined, PowerShell's switch table is one walk and a native argument that Windows PowerShell 5.1 would split is unreadable, and a single-slash switch is read only under the PowerShell tool with no prefix and no find clause in front of it. The same pass adds the eight-second deadline, the extra launchers, the stdin-path and carriage-return refusals, sudo's assignments, and rule 8's look through a wrapper.

1. `_find_clauses` (with `_check_word_list`) takes each `-exec`, `-execdir`, `-ok` or `-okdir` clause as the words up to its own `;`, or a `+` directly after `{}`, and checks that list once, including a nested `find`; no find clause is joined into text and read again.
2. `_read_cmd` refuses `@`, `;`, `,` and `=` anywhere in the inner command, `_walk_to_command` takes no `NAME=` assignment when the mode is cmd, and `_cmd_name_refusal` compares the command word's part before its first `.` in lower case without `base()` or a split on `/` or `\`.
3. `shell_inner` refuses a cmd word that is empty or holds a blank or a tab before the words after `/c`, `/k` or `/r` are joined.
4. `_read_powershell` refuses a word that begins with a quote and continues after the closing quote, a word that holds a literal `"` after quote removal, a word that begins with a single `-` and holds a `.`, and an unquoted `&` after a word has started.
5. `_ps_switch_walk` is the only reader of the PowerShell switch table: `_parse_ps_wrapper` and `shell_inner` both take their answer from that walk, a switch is a whole table name or a prefix of `-command`, `-file` is only `-f` or `-file`, a switch holding `=` is unreadable, and `PS_VERBS` includes `ConvertFrom` and `ConvertTo`.
6. `_cmd_switch_walk` and `_ps_switch_walk` read a single-slash switch only when `slash_ok` is set, which `check` sets only for the PowerShell tool in the PowerShell reading, and `_walk_to_command` clears it after a prefix wrapper; a find clause and every other reading pass `slash_ok` false, so only `//x` (and `-x` for powershell) is read there.
7. `_parse_bash_sh_words` refuses a `+` flag whose letters include `c`, and still accepts `+x`, `+e` and the other letters of the table.
8. `_stdin_script`, called from `_bash_reads_stdin`, refuses a script word `-`, and a path that is absolute or holds a `..` part when any part split on `/` and `\` (empty parts and `.` dropped) is `dev` or `proc`.
9. `_read_bash` treats a carriage return as unreadable outside quotes and comments, and still keeps a carriage return that stands directly after a backslash as a character of the word.
10. `_command_changes`, used by `_stage_changes`, treats a stage as changing state when its own command does or when a command of its `eval`, `bash`/`sh`, `cmd`, `powershell` or find-clause re-read does, and `chain_reason` applies the `cd` rule only to a list of two or more parts.
11. `_walk_to_command` calls `_take_assignments` after `sudo` and its options, so `HUSKY=0` and a `GIT_CONFIG` name are read as leading assignments.
12. `REFUSED_CMD_WORDS` adds `forfiles`, `conhost`, `schtasks`, `chroot`, `wt`, `git-bash` and `git-cmd`, compared with a trailing `.exe` removed.
13. `check` stores `_clock()` in `_read_started` when it starts; `_read_bash`, `_read_powershell`, `_read_cmd` and `_check_word_list` call `_too_long` at least once per command and once per 4096 characters, and refuse with `cannot read` and `too long to read in time`; `premerge_recorded` bounds the git `timeout` by `_time_left` and returns false when no time is left.
14. `_brace_in_word_ok` and `_parse_env_words` are deleted; nothing called them.
