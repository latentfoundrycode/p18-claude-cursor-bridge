# BUG-038: the completion check reports its own docstring as a stand-in

## Objective
Fix the following confirmed defect: repair the named cause and nothing else. Cause: the module docstring of `bridge/cursor-bridge/completion-check.py` has a STAND-IN paragraph (lines 29-32) that quotes the phrases its stand-in scan `STANDIN_RX` (lines 49-57) matches. The bridge ships this program as tracked, non-test code, so in `--mode done` the scan reads its own description as a stand-in, and the bridge's own project end can never pass. Evidence: at project end on the bridge itself (2026-10-31), the installed `completion-check.py --mode done` failed with two STAND-IN lines, `bridge/cursor-bridge/completion-check.py:30` and `:31`. Both are lines of that docstring paragraph. Line 30 (`stage", "coming soon", "not yet implemented/available", "under construction",`) matches `coming\s+soon`, `not\s+yet\s+(implemented|...)` and `under\s+construction`. Line 31 (`a *Placeholder{View,Page,Screen,Tab,Panel} component, todo!()/unimplemented!().`) matches `\btodo!\s*\(` and `\bunimplemented!\s*\(`. The scan works line by line (`for n, line in enumerate(text.splitlines(), 1): if STANDIN_RX.search(line):`, lines 191-195). The fix: reword only that docstring paragraph so that no line of the program matches `STANDIN_RX`, while the paragraph still names every kind of stand-in the scan looks for. Reproduction: `tests/test_bug038.py`, written by the supervisor before your run. Read the file whole before you start. It imports its helpers from `tests/test_completion_check.py`. On the current tree, `test_bug038_the_completion_check_finds_no_stand_in_in_its_own_source` and `test_bug038_a_stand_in_elsewhere_is_still_found` fail and must pass. `test_bug038_the_docstring_still_names_each_kind_it_scans_for` passes and must keep passing: it guards what the docstring means.

Satisfies: none. This is a defect fix to the bridge's own tooling. No requirement in docs/REQUIREMENTS.md is added or changed.

## Requirements
1. No line of `bridge/cursor-bridge/completion-check.py` matches `STANDIN_RX` (searched line by line, as the program's scan does).

The STAND-IN paragraph still names each kind of stand-in the scan looks for, in plain words a reader understands: a feature said to arrive later or to come soon; one not yet implemented or not yet available; one under construction; a placeholder component with the suffix View, Page, Screen, Tab, Panel or Section (Section is new: the scan already matches it, but the docstring did not list it); and Rust's `todo` and `unimplemented` macros.

5. Requirement 1 is met by wording, not by evasion. Do not split a matched phrase across a line break (the HEAD text already does this with "arrives in a later" / "stage"). Do not misspell words, add stray punctuation inside a phrase, or use escapes or non-ASCII look-alikes. Watch the forms that match even when quoted or described: "coming soon", "not yet implemented", "not yet available", "not implemented yet", "to be implemented", "under construction", "arrive(s) in a later stage/version/release/update", a word ending in `Placeholder` directly followed by one of the six suffixes (e.g. `PlaceholderView`), and a macro name directly followed by `!` and `(`.
