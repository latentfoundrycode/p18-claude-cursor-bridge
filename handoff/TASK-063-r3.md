## Objective
Rounds 1 and 2 tried to read the whole of bash's grammar by hand; both were rejected and both lost refusals the guard made at the checkpoint fae0860. ## Requirements
1. **One reader, a positive grammar.** (`words(command)` returns the words of the reading; on an unreadable reading it returns the empty list, and every caller of `words()`, `state_changing` among them, handles an empty list without raising.) One function reads the command once, left to right, with an explicit stack (no recursion that grows with the input), in time linear in its length (no regular expression over the whole line or a growing buffer, no slicing or joining per character, no scan of the stack per character). Every state names what it accepts; anything else returns the unreadable refusal. Every caller (`split_commands`, `chain_parts`, rule 8, `words()`, `commit_message`, the graphql test, the `gh api` `&` check, the HUSKY check) takes its commands and words from this reading; no other scanner of quotes, no second tokenizer and no shlex remain.
2. **The subset** (exactly the decision's list):
   - words: plain characters; single quotes (no escape); double quotes (a backslash escapes only `"`, `\`, `` ` ``, `$` and a newline); a backslash outside quotes making the next character literal; adjacent quoted and plain parts one word, quotes removed (`--fo"rce"` is `--force`); a whole-word `$'...'` (outside double quotes) decoded as bash decodes it, every escape with its digit counts, a decoded NUL refusing; a whole-word `$"..."` read as double quotes;
   - `$(...)`: its body read as commands with fresh quoting; an unquoted `(` inside it opens a level its `)` closes; `<(...)` and `>(...)` read the same, outside quotes only (inside quotes they are text);
   - subshells: an unquoted `(` at a command's start (top level or inside a body) opens a subshell level its `)` closes, its body read as a list of commands; after the `)` only blanks, a redirection, a separator or another `)` may follow; `(git add -A) && (git commit -m x)` stays rule 8's refusal (`tests/test_permission_guard_a4.py`, line 142);
   - backtick pairs: the end found first, at the first backtick not preceded by a backslash; the body unescaped (`\$` to `$`, `\\` to `\`, and inside double quotes `\"` to `"`) and then read as commands; a `` \` `` inside the body (a nested backtick) is unreadable;
   - separators at every level: `&&`, `||`, `;`, `|`, a new line, and every single `&` that is not part of a redirection; an empty command (blank lines, a leading `&` as PowerShell's call operator, a separator after another) is skipped, not refused;
   - a backslash before a new line (an odd run of backslashes) joins the lines outside single quotes, outside a comment and outside a quoted here-document's body, and never under PowerShell; a body line of an unquoted here-document that ends in an odd run of backslashes is unreadable (bash joins it before comparing the delimiter); round 2's `_join_continuations` (permission-guard.py line 1281), which joins whatever the quotes and comments, is removed: the join is the reader's;
   - redirections, each taken out of the words with its target wherever it stands in a simple command (before the command name too, as leading assignments are): an optional descriptor number before `<`, `>`, `>>`, `>|`, `<>`, `<&m`, `>&m`, `>&-`, and `&>`, `&>>`; the target read as a word, a substitution in it read as a command; the `&` inside `>&2` or `&>` is no separator;
   - a `#` that starts a word, outside quotes, begins a comment to the end of its line, a backslash before that new line included (inside a backtick body, to the end of its line or of the body, whichever comes first);
   - the standalone words `{` and `}`; leading assignments; the existing wrappers;
   - here-documents: an operator `<<`, optional blanks, then one plain word (`[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_]*`) or one wholly quoted plain word (`'EOF'`, `"EOF"`), at top level or inside `$(` only (a here-document inside another here-document's body, inside a substitution of an unquoted body or inside `<(`/`>(` is unreadable); the rest of the operator line, from the `<<` to its new line, read in the current context: every quote and substitution opened after the operator closes before that new line, and no backslash-newline stands there (what was opened before the operator, the app's `"$(cat <<'EOF'`, and a backslash-newline before it, the continued `gh pr create`, are not affected); the body is the following lines up to the line that is exactly the delimiter; a body line that begins with the delimiter but is not exactly it is unreadable; a `$(` that holds a here-document must not close on the operator line; a body with an unquoted delimiter is read with the double-quote rules (`$(`, backticks and `\$`, `` \` ``, `\\` read; `"` plain text); a quoted body is data unless a shell reads it: a wrapper shell as the command holding the `<<`, or as a later stage of the same pipeline, makes its lines commands; the here-document's first line stays available to `commit_message`.
3. **Unreadable, each refused with a reason holding the words `cannot read` and naming the form:** `<<-`; `<<<`; a second `<<` on one line; a here-document inside backticks; a here-document delimiter that is not one plain word or one wholly quoted plain word; `case` as a command word, `((`, `$((`; `${...}` holding a quote, a substitution, a backtick or a brace (a plain `${name}`, `${name:-word}`, `${#name}`, `${!name}` stay readable); an ANSI-C or locale string that is not a whole word; an unquoted brace expansion (a word holding `{...}` with an unquoted `,` or `..` inside; `HEAD@{1}`, `{}` and `{a}` stay readable); `{var}>` descriptors; an unclosed quote, substitution, backtick or here-document; a nesting past the bound of 64 (the reader's stack and `check`'s own recursion through eval, wrappers, `find -exec` and `xargs` together, one shared counter), its reason holding both `cannot read` and `nest`; a `{name}` before any redirection operator.
4. **The tool.** `check(command, cwd=None, tool="Bash")`; recursive calls keep the outer tool. Under the PowerShell tool the command is read **once**, by the subset with these changes: a backslash is a plain character (no escape, no join), and a backslash before a quote is unreadable; a backtick outside single quotes continues the line when a new line follows it and is unreadable anywhere else (PowerShell reads it as an escape: `` git pu`s`h `` is `push`); `@'`, `@"`, `<#` and a carriage return not followed by a new line are unreadable; there are no backtick substitutions. The inner command of a `powershell` or `pwsh` wrapper is read the PowerShell way under either tool. Round 2's double reading (`check` reading under PowerShell both ways) is removed.
5. **Rule 8.** Each list is read on its own: the top level, each substitution's body and each subshell's body; a pipeline is one part. A line is refused as a chain when some list has two or more parts and one of that list's parts changes state (it, or a command inside one of its substitutions or its subshell). Never flatten a body's parts into its parent's. The `cd` rule reads the top level only. A `gh api` read still counts as a state change.
6. **`HUSKY=0`.** fae0860's rule kept per simple command: a leading `HUSKY=0` assignment (after other assignments, and after `env` with its options and their values) refuses when that command's text holds `git` followed by `commit`, `push` or `merge` anywhere, wrappers' and `-c` strings' bodies included; and the same assignment carried into `check`'s recursion. Every form in the acceptance file's HUSKY cases refuses; `git commit -m "set HUSKY=0 in CI"` passes.
7. **The checkpoint subject** is read only from that commit command's own first `-m`/`--message` word, or from the here-document that word's substitution holds; never from another command's substitution (`echo $(echo checkpoint before TASK-001) ; git commit --no-verify -F msg.txt` refuses).
8. **The graphql test** reads the `gh api graphql` command's own words: refused when one of them holds a substitution the reader finds; `gh api graphql -f query='$(x)'` passes (single-quoted text); a substitution in another command of the line does not refuse it.
9. **The `gh api` `&` check** is a property of the reading: a `gh api` command (its head `gh`, then `api` after gh's options, `-R`/`--repo` and its value included) whose last word runs straight into an unquoted single `&` that the reader takes as a separator is refused with a reason that says to quote the argument (the word `quote` in it), before rule 8's reason. An escaped `\&` is part of the word and passes; redirections and `&&` are not this refusal.
10. **Every refusal of fae0860 and of the existing tests stays**: every case of `tests/test_permission_guard.py` and `tests/test_permission_guard_a4.py` passes unchanged (they hold fae0860's refusals); `settings.bridge.json` unchanged.
